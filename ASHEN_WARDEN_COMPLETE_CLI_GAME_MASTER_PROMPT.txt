1. PRODUCT VISION AND DESIGN PILLARS
------------------------------------
Genre: Single-player, dark-fantasy, turn-based tactical narrative RPG with Metroidvania-style ability gates translated to a text map.
Audience: Players who like Blasphemous-style gothic atmosphere, strategic boss battles, readable RPG stats, mystery, exploration, lore, and decisions.
Player fantasy: Become the last cursed Warden, uncover a betrayal that destroyed Veyrath, and choose what becomes of the dying Flame.
Game promise: Every turn, item, memory, location, enemy and choice should matter.

Pillars:
1) Player agency: command -> clear result -> updated state -> next choice. No forced combat actions.
2) Legible tactics: enemies telegraph intentions, strengths and weaknesses can be discovered, status effects are explained.

NON-GOALS: Real-time combat; moving characters frame by frame; ASCII art animation as a requirement; multiplayer; cloud login; online leaderboard; voice commands; AI-generated text at runtime; complex crafting simulation; procedurally generated main story.

2. THE COMPLETE NARRATIVE CANON
--------------------------------
WORLD: VEYRATH
Veyrath was a kingdom obsessed with conquering mortality. Beneath its holy halls burned an ancient power known as the Ashen Flame, capable of holding life against death, but incapable of restoring it without a price. The Order of Wardens existed to contain this power, answerable to its oath rather than to the crown. The royal court wanted to bind the Flame to the royal bloodline.

HISTORY / CHRONOLOGY
- Age of Embers: the Ashen Flame was found and worshipped, then feared.
- Age of Crowns: the independent Order of Wardens was established to guard and contain it.
- King's Decline: the king, desperate to escape death, plotted a forbidden binding ritual.
- Night of Seven Bells: the ritual required a living vessel. The Warden captain was chosen as the vessel; the Order opposed the ritual and assaulted the palace to stop it. The king betrayed the Warden captain, trying to seize the Flame for himself. The ritual shattered the kingdom's boundary between life and death. Seven bells sounded, seals fractured, and the curse swept through Veyrath.
- The long Ash: cities became tombs, clergy twisted into cults, knights into hollow shells, and the people lingered between life and oblivion.
- Present: as the seven seals weaken, the Warden awakens in a battlefield of ash with fractured memory, a silver sword, and a dying amber flame inside his chest.

IMPORTANT CANON RULE: The Warden and his Order did NOT knowingly support the king's ritual. Claims that the Warden willingly helped the king are propaganda or false recollections, revealed through memory collection. The Warden is not a conventional chosen-one hero: he is a betrayed vessel, survivor and possible source of liberation or renewed tyranny.

CENTRAL MYSTERY
Why did the Warden survive? Who ordered the ritual? Who was the intended vessel? What did the Order know? Can the Flame be extinguished without dooming the Warden? Is there a third path beyond death or tyranny? Reveal the answers gradually through the seven memories, NPC testimonies, recovered decrees, and final confrontation.

NARRATIVE STRUCTURE
PROLOGUE — THE TOMB OF COLD ASH
The Warden awakens, barely remembering his oath. Tutorial: inspect body, retrieve Warden's Silverblade, read a damaged journal, learn basic commands and battle a weakened Hollow Soldier. A distant bell rings. The Lantern Keeper hints that the Warden's heart carries the last ember. Goal: reach the Forgotten Outskirts.

ACT I — FORGOTTEN OUTSKIRTS / CATHEDRAL OF BROKEN BELLS
The player discovers a collapsed village, old soldiers and the first signs of a kingdom trapped after death. Meet the Lantern Keeper and the Ashen Smith. Recover the Memory of the Oath and Memory of the Warning. Enter the Cathedral of Broken Bells, uncover the bell ritual, defeat the Bell Keeper, and obtain Bellbreaker, which breaks marked cracked walls and reveals routes. Theme: loss, duty and the first evidence that the official account is wrong.

ACT II — BLACKWOOD FOREST / CRIMSON CATHEDRAL
A blighted forest conceals cults and specters. Introduce Wraith Step as an exploration/combat utility (earned through a quest or relic), discover clues to the ritual, and help or mistrust Sister Elara. In the Crimson Cathedral, confront the Crimson Bishop, whose dialogue repeats the false claim that the Wardens assisted the king. Defeat the Bishop, unlock Ashen Flame, and recover memories of Betrayal and Sacrifice. Theme: faith corrupted, truth hidden under propaganda.

ACT III — ROYAL CRYPTS
Descend through tombs and royal archives. Encounter the Fallen Executioner and Sir Vaelor, a former Warden whose role depends on choices and discovered memories. Uncover the Memory of the Vessel and the Memory of the Final Assault. Unlock the traversal ability called Gravebound Leap (the text-CLI adaptation of Double Jump), enabling elevated crypt routes; this is a command-based route unlock, not physical platforming. Theme: the protagonist's identity and the Order's last resistance.

ACT IV — PALACE OF THE HOLLOW CROWN
Reach the royal palace after assembling access sigils and confronting the last royal defenders. Recover the Memory of the Choice. Confront the Hollow King, who shifts through swordsmanship, cursed royal flame, and a final monstrous manifestation. Resolve the ending based on choices, restored seals, memory collection and final selection. Theme: whether survival, duty or power should define the last Warden.

THE SEVEN WARDEN MEMORIES
1. Oath — the Warden's promise to protect the Flame from misuse.
2. Warning — the Order learned of the king's dangerous intentions.
3. Betrayal — the king turned on the captain and the Order.
4. Sacrifice — comrades died trying to stop the ritual.
5. Vessel — the captain was selected to carry the Flame.
6. Final Assault — the Order attacked the palace to prevent the ritual.
7. Choice — the final insight into freeing the Flame from the bloodline.
Each memory has: ID, title, location/quest trigger, short cinematic text, expanded journal entry, permanent story flag, and any related dialogue unlocks. Show acquired memory count as 0/7 through 7/7. Do not award the same memory twice.

THREE ENDINGS
A) THE LAST EMBER — the Warden absorbs/extinguishes the Flame, undead souls rest, and the Warden ceases to exist. This is a valid sacrifice ending.
B) THE HOLLOW THRONE — the Warden claims the crown and becomes a new immortal ruler, replacing one tyrant with another. The curse persists in altered form.
C) DAWN BEYOND ASH — secret/true ending; requires all seven memories and the ancient seals restored, plus appropriate final choice. The Flame is freed from the royal bloodline, and the Warden survives without the curse. The player must see why the ending is locked if requirements are missing, without spoiling its narrative content too early.
Use a centralized EndingResolver; do not determine endings by scattered if-statements in UI code.

5. TURN-BASED COMBAT — EXACT BEHAVIOR
-------------------------------------
Combat begins when the player enters a hostile encounter or deliberately attacks an enemy. The screen shows player HP/stamina/Ash energy, equipped weapon, vial count, enemy name/HP, status effects, and enemy intent. The player must select an action. Invalid commands do NOT consume a turn. 'inspect', 'help', and 'status' do NOT consume a turn. Inventory inspection does not consume a turn; USING an item does. Equipment changes during combat should be forbidden or explicitly consume a turn, chosen consistently.

A COMBAT ROUND
1. At the beginning of the round, reveal a readable enemy intent (e.g., 'Heavy overhead slash', 'Blood spell charging', 'Defending', 'Summoning').
2. Display legal actions and their costs.
3. Wait for explicit user command; never auto-select.
4. Validate action, target, resource cost, cooldown, and any equipment restriction.
5. Apply player action, calculate damage/status/stagger/healing, append combat log.
6. If enemy defeated, resolve rewards, quest events, and victory; skip its action.
7. Otherwise resolve enemy action, including counterplay against defend/parry/interrupt.
8. Apply end-of-round effects in a documented order, decrement cooldowns, resolve deaths, and select next intent.
9. Repeat until victory, defeat or successful escape.

COMMANDS: attack, heavy, defend, parry, skill <name>, item <name>, inspect enemy, inspect item <name>, status, help, flee. Support numeric aliases.

STARTING BALANCE (TUNABLE, NOT CANONICAL)
Player level 1: HP 100, stamina 50, Ash energy 30, attack 10, defense 5, vials 3. Basic attack costs 0 stamina, deals 100% calculated weapon damage; heavy attack costs 15 stamina and deals ~160% plus stagger; defend reduces eligible incoming damage by 50%; parry counters telegraphed parryable melee, fails against spells/grabs/unblockables; a Crimson Vial restores 40 HP capped at max; flee is unavailable in boss fights and may cost a turn on failure. Stamina recovery occurs at turn end or by defend, defined explicitly. Skill costs vary. The first two enemies should be survivable without perfect decisions.

DAMAGE FORMULA GUIDANCE
Separate base attack, weapon attack, temporary bonuses, defense mitigation, elemental resistances, critical effects and status application. Never permit negative damage; minimum 1 for a successful non-immune damaging hit. Example: raw = attackStat + weaponPower + skillBonus; mitigated = max(1, raw - floor(defense * 0.5)); apply action multiplier and resistance; clamp and round consistently. Use an injectable seeded Random for crits, loot and flee, never Math.random scattered across classes. Display effective damage and causes, not internal random seed.

DEFEND/PARRY/INTENT
Defend works against ordinary attacks but not all special attacks. Parry only works against tagged 'PARRYABLE' attacks; on success, block/counter and inflict stagger. Some enemies feint or charge, but never make their telegraph intentionally false without a discoverable mechanic. 'Inspect enemy' reveals known resistances and whether the current attack is parryable. Telegraph clarity is more important than artificial difficulty.

STATUS EFFECTS
BLEED: damage over turns; BURN: flame damage over turns; POISON: damage and possible healing reduction; STUN: skip one action; WEAKNESS: reduced outgoing damage; GUARD BREAK: temporarily lowered defense; CURSE: special boss/debuff mechanic. Define duration, stack limits, refresh rules, immunity/resistance and update timing. Log each tick. Prevent status effects from persisting forever accidentally.

ENEMY BEHAVIOR: STRICTLY RULE-BASED, NO AI
Each enemy has a small explicit priority table/state machine. Example: Hollow Soldier below 25% HP may defend if not recently defended; otherwise if player is defending use shield bash; otherwise light slash or charged heavy. Blood Cultist casts if energy available; otherwise gathers energy or retreats. Bosses change movesets at HP thresholds. Seeded randomness may choose between valid actions but no ML/LLM.

BOSS DESIGN
BELL KEEPER: fused with giant bell and chains; slow chain swings, area shockwaves, bell toll causing stagger. Counterplay: defend shockwave, exploit wind-up, stagger after a missed chain strike. Reward: Bellbreaker and progress flag.
CRIMSON BISHOP: blood magic, projectile orbs, cultist summons, teleport and blood apparition phase. Counterplay: interrupt channeling, cleanse bleeding, defeat summoned cultists. Reward: Ashen Flame.
HOLLOW KING: phase 1 royal sword techniques; phase 2 flame pillars, teleport, crown beam; phase 3 supernatural flame entity with escalating curse pressure. Final dialogue and ending choice after combat.
OPTIONAL BOSSES: Mourning Knight (mirrors Warden sword techniques); Pale Widow (spectral illusions, spectral resistances); Ash Serpent (large burning beast, area attacks). Optional boss rewards must be useful but not required for a standard ending.

8. DATABASE / JDBC PERSISTENCE — NON-NEGOTIABLE
-----------------------------------------------
Use local MySQL 8+ and JDBC with MySQL Connector/J. Maven dependency pinned to a documented current compatible version; use environment variables DB_URL, DB_USER, DB_PASSWORD (with safe localhost URL default only). Do not commit credentials. Support first-run setup with clear message if database missing or inaccessible; do not crash with a raw stack trace.

SCHEMA GUIDELINES
- schema_version / migrations table.
- players (character identity and non-slot-specific metadata, if any).
- save_slots (slot ID, player ID or character ID, chapter, current location, sanctuary, time, updated_at, game version).
- player_state (SAVE-SPECIFIC current stats, currency, experience, ability flags; primary key save_id).
- item_definitions (immutable catalogue keyed by stable item_code).
- inventory_entries (save_id, item_code, quantity, upgrade level where applicable; unique or instance-based gear as needed).
- equipped_items (save_id, slot, inventory_entry_id or stable item reference).
- region_definitions and location_definitions (optional if static content is loaded from files; do not duplicate mutable source of truth).
- discovered_locations (save_id, location_code).
- unlocked_paths (save_id, path_code).
- quest_definitions (optional static catalogue).
- quest_progress (save_id, quest_code, status, objective progress, reward_claimed).
- story_flags (save_id, flag_code, value).
- acquired_memories (save_id, memory_code).
- defeated_bosses (save_id, boss_code).
- optional encounter_state (save_id, enemy type/HP/status/turn/seed) only if mid-combat save is supported.
- optional combat_history / journal_entries for readable history; cap logs to avoid uncontrolled growth.

KEY DATABASE RULE: Save slots MUST be independent. Never key inventory and mutable player state only by a shared player ID if multiple save slots represent distinct playthroughs. Use save_id consistently for slot-specific state. Foreign keys, UNIQUE constraints and CHECK constraints where supported. Index save_id and commonly joined keys. No credentials or SQL strings embedded in CLI renderer.

DAO/REPOSITORY LAYER
DatabaseConnection or DataSource factory; TransactionManager; SaveRepository; PlayerRepository; InventoryRepository; EquipmentRepository; QuestRepository; WorldProgressRepository; BossProgressRepository; StoryRepository. Repositories expose Java objects, not ResultSet to the game engine. Use PreparedStatement for all parameters. Use try-with-resources. Keep SQL in repository classes. Prefer batch inserts for save snapshots where sensible.

SAVE TRANSACTION
1. Validate that saving is permitted.
2. Begin JDBC transaction with autoCommit(false).
3. Upsert save metadata and player state.
4. Persist inventory/equipment, quest progress, location discovery, story flags, memories, boss defeats and any allowed encounter state.
5. Commit only if all operations succeed; otherwise rollback.
6. Display success only AFTER commit. On error, retain in-memory game state, show useful retry guidance, do not claim save succeeded.

LOAD TRANSACTION
Load a coherent snapshot, validate referential integrity and version compatibility, rebuild derived stats from equipment, restore current location and unlocked routes, and report a helpful error for corrupt or unsupported saves. Do not reset quest rewards or respawn defeated bosses. Offer multiple named save slots and last-updated timestamp. A 'new game' creates an independent save record, not an overwrite without confirmation.

TRANSACTIONAL GAME ACTIONS
Purchases, sales, upgrades and quest rewards involving currency + inventory must be atomic when persisted immediately. For in-memory-first design, save snapshot transaction still must be atomic; decide and document which operations autosave. Do not mix unsynchronized direct SQL writes and in-memory mutations without a strategy. Recommended: game state is authoritative during play; repositories persist at checkpoints/save events; for shop actions optionally transactionally persist immediately and update in-memory state only after success.

SECURITY/ROBUSTNESS
Parameterized SQL, bounded input lengths, no SQL injection, no password in source or logs, graceful DB connection failure, no silent data loss. Handle duplicate records, negative quantities, invalid slot IDs, disconnected MySQL and interrupted writes. Keep migration scripts under src/main/resources/db/migration. Include example environment setup and MySQL user with minimum required privileges.

10. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
-------------------------
Architecture style: modular monolith with presentation -> application services -> domain -> persistence separation.

CLI Input -> CommandParser -> CommandDispatcher -> GameEngine / GameSession
    -> CombatService / ExplorationService / QuestService / InventoryService
    -> Domain: Player, Enemy, Item, Location, Quest, GameState
    -> Repository Interfaces -> JDBC Implementations -> MySQL
CLI Renderer <- typed GameEvent / CommandResult / ViewModel

DOMAIN DESIGN
- GameState: the single authoritative in-memory session snapshot; current save ID, player, location, quest flags, memories, boss defeats, inventory, equipment, ability unlocks, encounter state, RNG seed if needed.
- GameEngine: owns game phase and transitions (MAIN_MENU, EXPLORATION, DIALOGUE, SHOP, COMBAT, LOOT, REST, GAME_OVER, ENDING).
- CommandParser: turns raw input into typed command and arguments; no business logic.
- CommandDispatcher: checks current phase and routes command to service.
- CommandResult: status, events, next phase, optional prompt and error.
- CombatEngine: turn resolution, damage, status and rewards; asks EnemyBehavior for an action, never asks an LLM.
- EnemyBehavior: deterministic rule tables/state machine, seeded RNG if necessary.
- WorldGraph: locations and exits; evaluates traversal requirements.
- QuestManager: consumes domain events (enemy defeated, item obtained, NPC spoken to, location visited, memory acquired).
- Item/EquipmentService: validates actions, calculates derived stats and sends result events.
- SaveService: coordinates a complete transaction across repositories.
- TerminalRenderer: pure presentation; must not modify GameState or call SQL.

OOP CONCEPTS TO DEMONSTRATE
Encapsulation: private fields and validated state transitions.
Inheritance: abstract Combatant or shared base where appropriate; do not force deep hierarchies.
Polymorphism: weapon skill strategies, item effects, enemy behaviors, command handlers.
Abstraction/interfaces: Combatant, EnemyBehavior, GameCommand, ItemEffect, Repository.
Composition: Player has Inventory/Equipment; Location has Exits/Interactables; GameEngine has services.
Exception handling: domain errors, invalid commands, database failures, missing data.
Collections/DSA: graph adjacency map for world, Map for inventory and quest state, queue/event list for game events, enums for states, ordered list for combat log. Use algorithms that are justified by gameplay rather than unnecessary complexity.

DATA-DRIVEN CONTENT
Prefer data tables or simple UTF-8 resources for long dialogues, lore, enemy configurations, items and locations rather than massive hardcoded switch statements. If using JSON, add a small documented parser dependency or use properties/TSV files with strict parsing; avoid implementing an error-prone custom JSON parser just to avoid dependencies. MySQL stores mutable save state; static game definitions can live in version-controlled resources, with stable IDs in save tables. Do not store raw Java serialized objects in SQL.

15. QUALITY, SAFETY AND IMPLEMENTATION RULES
---------------------------------------------
- Always compile after adding a meaningful feature; run automated tests before merging.
- Prefer small cohesive classes and clear names over a 2000-line Game.java.
- Keep all random behavior seedable and combat formulas in one place.
- Never use hardcoded absolute Windows paths; use cross-platform resources and relative paths.
